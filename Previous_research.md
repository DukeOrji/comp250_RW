**Ethics Behind Social Media Automated Content Moderation**

There are multiple aspects of of AI based content moderation, mainly adversarial vulnerabililties, human involvement in automated moderation and the social consequences of AI decision making.

Adversarial vulnerabilities focuses on how attackers manipulate inputs to evade automated detection systems and how machine learning models can be modified to be capable of detecting these attacks.
Chen and Ji (2022) examine feature level adversarial training as a method of improving model robustness.
Muthalagu, Malik, and Pawar (2024) examined evasion attacks and the dangers it pose to moderation systems, while Zhou, He,and Sun (2024) and Xiang et al. (2025) both focus on how AI generated text detection sytems fail to detect paraphrased AI generated text and slightly modified text like sarcasm.
Yang et al. (2026) showcases how mainpualtion of harmful text enables it to evade LLM powered moderation systems.
Collectively these studies assert that automated content moderation systems remain unreliable  and vulnerable to adversarial manipulation, this exact issue makes continous adverarial testing and modifications to model framework to improve its robustness relevant to this project.
The second aspect of AI based content moderation is its effectivenes with human inclusion and transparency.
Weldon (2026) and Janicki (2025) experiment on content moderation using different moderation techniques such as combining automated systems with human review, especially when examining ambigous or complex cases such as context dependent content.
Molina and Sundar (2022) analyze the benefits human collaboration combined with model transparency poses especially to the public, they suggest it would influence user trust and improve user's understanding and sense of autonomy.
So far all these studies point to whether human oversight would be more beneficial than allowing AI systems to independently make decisions.
Another major aspect of AI based automated content moderation focuses on its social and ethical consequences particularly regarding system behavior.
Uveges(2024) highlights how false positives can prevent legitimate expression while false negatives would allow harmful content to be accessible to the public, thses are competing concequences of moderation misclassification.
The New York Times (2026) reports on unexpected AI behavior where AI systems where acting outside of their intended constraints/regulations, this raises serious concerns about AI reliability and trust.
To conclude, my resesarch covers the social concequences, technical vulnerabililties and limitation of automated content decision making.
However it also proposes the need improve the defense mechanisms of automated moderation systems through continous adversarial testing and inclusion of human oversight.
